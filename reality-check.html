Last Chair Studio · Reality Check

I tracked four Survivor leagues in a Figma file. Then I created the app that replaced it.

Reality Check runs a fantasy league around a reality show. You draft cast members, call who goes home each week, and talk about it in a league chat. Four leagues of friends and family have played it every week this season. I watched what they asked, what they missed and what they requested, and designed from that.

How I tested it

My testers are my friends and family. That’s a real limit, and I had to be honest with myself about what it meant.

They’re playing to win, not to test. They like me. And when something goes wrong they assume they did it. So in a full season nobody told me a screen was confusing.

If I’d waited for feedback, I’d have learned nothing. So I stopped listening for opinions and watched for three things I could trust.

What I watched for

Questions. If someone asks in the chat, the screen didn’t answer it.

Behavior. A missed pick, or a text asking me to reopen one.

Requests. People asked for GIFs, more than once. They loved the games. Before the draft, bios and intro videos helped them put names to faces.

One request changed the product. Our group text kept spoiling episodes, so chat moved into the app.

Where it started

It started as a Figma board

A picture of right now

I play in several Survivor leagues with family and friends. We draft in person, and for years I tracked it all in Figma.

It took me a few seasons to see why it kept breaking. A Figma board is a picture of right now. A league is a list of things that happened.

So the app does the opposite. I enter what happened in an episode once. Standings, scores and who’s still in are all worked out from that.

One thing carried over. Standings in the app show each player’s picks as faces, and the ones who are out fade back. That’s the Figma board, and it was right the first time.

Decision 1

“Which week is this?”

What I saw

My mom, a family friend and AJ all did the same thing more than once. They thought they’d made their pick for the week. After the episode aired they found out they hadn’t.

The family league plays for money, so a missed pick costs something.

The obvious fix was already there

A reminder went out 12 hours before every episode. They got it, and they still missed.

So this wasn’t a forgetting problem. They had picked. They’d picked for the wrong episode, and the app let them believe they were done.

Why it happened

The app was organized by feature. A tab called Predictions held this week’s pick, the season pick and old weeks on one page. “I made a pick” felt true no matter which one you’d touched.

And the app ran on two clocks. Picks lock before the episode airs, so the app had already moved to next week while everyone was still living in this one.

I made the episode the unit

I stopped organizing by feature. Four changes followed from that.

  1. Every episode got its own page. Picks, results and the recap live together. If you want to know what you picked, you go to that episode.
  2. The app moves forward when the episode is posted, not when a timer runs out. My first rule was “open next week 24 hours after air”. A timer is a guess, and it guessed wrong right away: Amazing Race episode 4 opened for picks while episode 3 still had no results.
  3. One lock time, written on the screen. Lock time followed each phone’s clock, so my Mountain and Pacific leagues locked later than the East Coast one. With money involved, that’s unfair. Now it’s one hour before the Eastern air time for everyone, and the screen says “7 PM ET”.
  4. The pick moved to the top of Home. It’s the only thing in the app with a deadline, so it goes first. And a filled-in dropdown looks finished, so nothing saves until you tap “Lock in picks”.

What it cost: standings are the fun part, and they now sit lower on Home. I decided a missed pick is worse than one more scroll.

What happened

The missed picks stopped. AJ and several others told me it’s a lot clearer now.

Decision 2

The editor follows the show

This time I was the confused user

The editor was one long page, ordered by what the scoring cared about most. Who went home came first, because that’s the biggest number. The episode title came near the end.

But I fill it in right after watching, and I remember an episode the way it aired. The title, then camp, then the challenge, then Tribal. The screen made me jump around in my own memory, and that’s when I’d skip something.

A mistake here isn’t small. What I enter updates every league playing that show.

I checked my own assumption

I laid out the steps in air order. Then I stopped, because “the order of an episode” was just my feeling as a fan. So I had it checked season by season against recent Survivor, plus The Amazing Race and The Traitors for later.

One finding changed the design. Seven of the last ten Survivor seasons had an episode with two Tribal Councils. My editor had one set of vote boxes. I’d been squeezing those episodes in and hoping.

  • One step at a time, in air order: Opening, Tribes, Challenges, Found and won, Tribal, Recap, Review.
  • Each Tribal Council is its own step, showing only the people who were there.
  • Steps that don’t apply go away. On the old page every section was always open, so every section was a question: did this happen? Now I’m asked less.
  • Review is the last step, because of what a mistake costs. It shows what each person will score before I save.

What I got wrong: my first order had one step for all advantages. It was tidy, and it broke my own rule. Finding an idol happens at camp. Playing it happens at Tribal. They became two.

What I changed about how I review

The first audit of the new editor passed. Then I entered a real episode and found five problems it had missed. Looking at a screen and using a screen are different tests. Now nothing counts as reviewed until it’s been used start to finish.

Decision 3

One person, several leagues

Three bugs that were one problem

  • A notification arrives. Which league is it from, and where does tapping it go?
  • I read this week’s recap in one league. Why is it still “new” in my other league for the same show?
  • I want every chat message from one league and none from another.

I’d built the app as if everything belonged to a league, and it doesn’t.

  • You. Your name, your photo, light or dark.
  • A league. The chat, the standings, your notification settings.
  • A show. The episodes, the results, the recap.

Each bug was something sitting at the wrong level. The recap belongs to the show, so reading it once should clear it everywhere. Chat belongs to the league, so its settings do too.

Where it is now

  • Reading an episode in one league marks it read in your others for that show.
  • Notifications are set per league and clear once you’ve seen the thing. Message previews start off, because anyone can read a lock screen.
  • The switcher stayed in the header, because people already understood it there. A dot on a league says why it’s there.
  • Switching keeps you on the same tab. You changed league. You didn’t change what you were doing.

What’s left: I found this model by fixing bugs, which is backwards. The next step is to design from it. The first thing it doesn’t answer yet: a season ends, but a league has no “over” state, so past leagues have nowhere to go.

Accessibility

Dark mode that passes contrast

I tested the worst case, not the average

Dark mode sits on a dusk sky, and the cards are see-through so the sky shows behind them.

That breaks the normal way of checking contrast. A contrast check compares two colors. Here the background isn’t one color. It changes with where the card sits on the sky.

So I took real screenshots and measured the text over the lightest, pinkest part of the sky. Grey hint text came out at 4.2:1, so I darkened it to 4.9:1.

Large text gave me a rule

I ran the whole app at a large accessibility size and four things broke. They all had the same cause: text inside a fixed shape, like initials in a circle. So the rule is that what’s inside a fixed shape stays put, and the text next to it grows.

Still to do: a full VoiceOver run on a real phone.

How I work with AI

I describe it, review it on a phone and send it back

I didn’t draw these screens in Figma. I described what I wanted, looked at the build on a phone, and sent changes back until it matched what I had in my head.

AI is fast at making a screen that looks fine. Looking fine and being right aren’t the same, and telling them apart is the part that’s still mine. A typical note from me:

“the who you think goes home each week is way too long, maybe it’s just a dropdown”

That became the pick screen above.

What the speed bought me was the method. A problem I saw on Wednesday night was fixed before the next Wednesday, so I could try something, watch one episode and know.

Looking back

What I’d do differently

Test outside my circle sooner

Everyone who has used this can text me when something’s off, and that hides problems. A stranger just leaves.

Learn the plumbing first

One background job read too much data and cost me about $91 before I caught it. I’d rather have known what costs money before I designed something that ran every minute.

Expect silence on polish

I thought people would notice the type and the motion. Nobody has mentioned either. People notice when they miss a pick. That told me where my time should go first.
